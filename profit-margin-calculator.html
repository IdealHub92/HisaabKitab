Margin vs. markup — what's the difference?

These two terms get mixed up constantly, and the difference actually matters for pricing decisions. Margin is your profit as a percentage of the selling price — it tells you what portion of each sale is profit. Markup is your profit as a percentage of the cost price — it tells you how much you added on top of what you paid.

For example, if something costs you Rs 100 and you sell it for Rs 150: your profit is Rs 50. Your margin is 33% (50 ÷ 150), but your markup is 50% (50 ÷ 100). Using the wrong one when setting prices is a common way small businesses accidentally underprice their products.

  • If you're aiming for a specific margin, calculate the required markup separately — they're not interchangeable.
  • Factor in all costs, not just the direct purchase cost — packaging, delivery, and wastage all eat into your real margin.
  • Compare your margin against your industry's typical range, since acceptable margins vary widely by sector (retail vs. food service vs. wholesale, for instance).
