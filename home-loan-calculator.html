How home financing works in Pakistan

Banks offer home financing in two broad forms: conventional interest-based mortgages, and Islamic home financing (commonly Diminishing Musharakah, where the bank and buyer jointly own the property and the buyer gradually purchases the bank's share). Both typically result in a similar-looking fixed monthly payment.

  • Most banks require 10-30% down payment depending on the property and financing scheme.
  • Stretching a loan from 15 to 25 years lowers your installment but can roughly double total interest paid — calculate both before committing.
  • Factor in property transfer fees, stamp duty, and legal charges separately.

Frequently asked questions

Conventional vs Islamic home financing — which is calculated here?

This uses a standard reducing-balance formula, which closely matches the payment amount for both conventional mortgages and Islamic Diminishing Musharakah structures, even though the underlying legal structure differs.

Is a longer tenure always a bad idea?

Not necessarily — it depends on your priorities. A longer tenure lowers your monthly installment (helpful for affordability) but roughly doubles total interest paid over 15-25 years. Calculate both before deciding.

What's not included in this calculation?

Property transfer fees, stamp duty, legal/documentation charges, and property insurance are not included — factor these in separately when budgeting.
